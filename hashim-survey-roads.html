X-SEC PRO · User guide

Welcome to X-SEC PRO

X-SEC PRO by Hashim Survey is a road study toolkit for land surveyors and engineers. It runs entirely in your web browser – nothing to install, no Civil 3D needed. With it you can:

  • Set out a road alignment from PI coordinates or by drawing on satellite imagery, with circular curves at every bend.
  • Enter or import your field levels (chainage · offset · level) and get the long section, cross sections, cut & fill volumes and mass haul.
  • Design the grade line, check it against design criteria, and register bridges, culverts, pipes and junctions for engineering review.
  • Export everything to PDF reports, Excel (with live formulas), AutoCAD DXF, Civil 3D LandXML, KML, CSV and GeoJSON.
  • Compute cut & fill and stockpile volumes between OGL and final levels inside a plot limit, with a colour depth map.
  • Digitize plots and roads into a vector layout plan, and work in local coordinate systems (DLTM, Nahrwan, Adindan, UTM and 6,000+ EPSG systems).
Your data stays on your computer. Files you open are read inside your browser – they are not uploaded anywhere. Only the background imagery and the free terrain model are downloaded from the internet.

Works best in Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge on a laptop or desktop. It also opens on tablets and phones for viewing.

Quick start – your first road in 5 minutes

The fastest way to learn is with the built-in example:

  1. Open the 🛣 Road study tab (it opens by default).
  2. Click 📝 Open levels table, then Load example. A sample level book appears with chainages, offsets and levels.
  3. Close the table with ✕ Close and press ▶ Road from levels. The road is drawn and the long section appears in the panel under the map.
  4. Look through the tabs under the map: Longitudinal profile, Cross sections, Setting-out, Volumes & mass haul, Features & review.
  5. Press ⬇ Report (PDF, A3) or ⬇ Excel (formulas) to see the deliverables.

For a real job, follow the Field workflow: alignment → stations & offsets → enter levels → road from levels → design → export.

Road study

The Road study tab is the heart of the program. You can work in two ways:

  • From your field levels (recommended for real jobs): the levels you measured are used exactly as entered – see Field workflow.
  • From a terrain model: draw or load the centreline and let the program read ground levels from the free global DEM, your survey points (TIN) or your own GeoTIFF.

Field workflow – A. Alignment & B. Stations

A. Alignment

Type one PI (point of intersection) per line in the box: PI1, 512345.120, 2680123.450 (Point, Easting, Northing, in the project coordinate system), then press Use these PIs.

Or press ✎ Draw on map and click each PI on the imagery; press Finish (or double-click) to end. The ⇄ button writes the current alignment's PIs back into the box as coordinates so you can correct them.

3. Horizontal curves & 4. Vertical alignment

Horizontal curves lists every bend: deflection angle, radius, tangent length, curve length and the chainages of TC (start), MC (middle) and CT (end). Change a radius in the table to update the road. ⚠ means the radius was reduced to fit between bends.

Vertical alignment lists the PVIs (grade-line break points) with chainage, level, grades in/out and vertical curve length / K value.

  • Auto grade line – fits a grade line that keeps the minimum height above ground and stays under the max. grade. If design levels were given in the levels table, those are used instead.
  • Auto curve lengths – sets vertical curve lengths from the design speed.
  • In the Longitudinal profile under the map: drag a PVI to move it, double-click to add one, right-click to delete.

Cut & fill / soil stock

The ⛰ Cut & fill tab compares the OGL (original ground levels) with a final surface inside a plot limit and gives the cut, fill and stockpile volumes, with a colour depth map.

  1. Plot limit – type the corners (C1, 226268.52, 2572690.99 one per line) and press Use these corners, draw it with ✎ Draw limit on map, pick a plot from the Vector map, drop a KML polygon, or use the outline of the OGL points.
  2. OGL – 📥 Load OGL file… (CSV, TXT or Excel: Point, Easting, Northing, Level) or paste the points.
  3. Final surface – choose one:
    • Final survey points – the as-built or stockpile survey. Tick “Outside the final survey, ground is unchanged” when you only surveyed the stockpile.
    • Flat formation level – type the level (e.g. 134.274).
    • Balance level – the program finds the level where cut × factor = fill.
  4. Set the cut → fill factor (1.00 = none; e.g. 0.90 if compacted fill needs more material) and press ▶ Compute cut & fill.
ResultMeaning
CUTVolume where the OGL is above the final surface (material removed).
FILL / STOCKVolume where the final surface is above the OGL (fill placed or stockpile).
Surplus / shortageCut × factor − fill. With final points: net added = fill − cut.
Depth mapRed = cut (+), blue = fill (−), black line = zero line, dashed lines = depth contours.

Exports: ⬇ Report (PDF, A3) (depth map, volumes, corner coordinates, table of every point), ⬇ Excel (formulas) (every grid cell with live formulas – with a formation level you can change the level in Excel and the volumes update), DXF (AutoCAD) (limit, zero line, depth contours, points and labels) and Points CSV.

Method: both surfaces are triangulated (TIN) and compared on a grid clipped to the plot limit; edge cells count only their area inside. The grid cell is chosen automatically (about 40,000 cells) or typed in.

Tips & common questions

My points appear in the wrong place (or in the sea).

The coordinate system is wrong. Set it in Geo data → Coordinate system before importing, or check the UTM zone in the coordinate grid. Also check that Easting and Northing are not swapped.

How does the program know the design level of each cross section?

From the grade line: if you gave design levels in the levels table they are used directly; otherwise the auto grade line (or your PVIs) gives the design level at every chainage, including vertical curves.

The import put my levels in the wrong columns.

In the import dialog correct the Header row and each column choice; switch Layout if your file has one row per chainage.

A button is grey.

It needs data first – for example exports are enabled after the road study has been run.

Will I lose my work if I close the browser?

Use 💾 Save project to keep a file. The last session can also be restored on the same computer with ↺ Restore last session.

Can I use it without internet?

The program needs internet to open and to show imagery and the free DEM. Your own levels, calculations and exports do not depend on online services.
